# Submission video — 2 to 3 minutes

Mandatory deliverable: a public or unlisted YouTube link, 2–3 minutes, showing the working project.
Record it **after code freeze and before submission**, while the app is known-good, then upload it
immediately. Re-record only if a real fix landed afterwards.

**Who:** one narrator (the clearest voice on the team), one person driving the mouse to the script.
Record the screen at 1080p or 720p, no webcam, no music.

**Prep before recording:** wifi off, `start.bat` already running, app on **Today** / **Velachery**,
browser zoom 100%, and a clean desktop with no personal notifications.

Timing budget: 0:00–0:25 hook · 0:25–1:05 the time machine · 1:05–1:50 the 3D moment ·
1:50–2:25 the wetland fix and the safe street · 2:25–2:45 close.

---

### Shot 1 — the hook (0:00–0:25)

On screen: the app on Chennai, dial showing 8.5, pin on the Velachery stop. Slow mouse drift over the
map so it feels alive.

> "Chennai floods. It flooded in 2005, it flooded in 2015, and it flooded again in 2020 — but until
> now there was no way to ask what that means for one street. This is the Flood Time Machine. Type any
> address in Chennai, and it tells you that address's flood risk, from 2005 all the way to 2070."

On-screen text: **Flood Time Machine · Chennai · 2005 → 2070**

---

### Shot 2 — one number with receipts (0:25–1:05)

Zoom out slightly so the coloured risk grid fills the frame, then zoom back to the pin.

> "Drag the years and the whole city re-scores. 2015 is the year everyone remembers — this is the
> actual satellite flood mapping from NRSC, three thousand seven hundred polygons, two hundred and
> fifty-five square kilometres of water."

Click **2015**, then point the cursor at the receipt line.

> "Every stop carries its receipts: which agency published it, how many polygons, and what the rain
> did that month. Nothing here was guessed by an AI — it is published data, and you can see the
> source."

---

### Shot 3 — the 3D moment (1:05–1:50)

Click **2070**, then press **3D**. Let it tilt. Hold still for two seconds.

> "Here is 2070 — the two-hundred-year flood corridor the city has already mapped. Now watch the
> water." *(tilt and water rise)*

> "That is real satellite terrain, and every block of water is that cell's modelled depth. We
> exaggerate it three times, and we say so on screen, because Chennai is almost flat."

Slowly rotate with the compass.

> "The buildings are real footprints from OpenStreetMap. Where a height is published we use it —
> where it is not, we show a flat massing and label it. We could have drawn a skyline. It would have
> been invented, and this is a risk product."

On-screen text: **Real terrain · real footprints · heights labelled**

---

### Shot 4 — the fix and the control (1:50–2:25)

Press **3D** off. Click **Taramani**.

> "Five-point-six. This street sits right beside the Pallikaranai marsh — the wetland that used to
> absorb this water."

Press **Restore wetlands**.

> "Put the marsh back, and the hazard drops. Five-point-six becomes four-point-seven. Same street,
> same rain, less risk."

Click **SRM Kattankulathur**.

> "And it tells you where not to worry. Our own campus, thirty-five metres up: one-point-four, even in
> 2070. That is what makes the other numbers believable."

Point at the exposure panel on the right.

> "And because a score alone is not an answer, every year comes with its count: in twenty-fifteen's
> water sat seven-point-four lakh people, ninety-two thousand buildings and three thousand kilometres
> of road. That is what a flood actually is — infrastructure with people on it."

---

### Shot 5 — close (2:25–2:45)

Turn wifi off on camera, or show the "offline" chip and refresh the page.

> "It runs from a folder on a laptop with no internet, because when your city floods, the towers go
> down first. Every tile, every score, every flood record ships inside the app. This is the Flood Time
> Machine."

On-screen text: **Flood Time Machine · Teen Titans · GEO IMPATHON 1.0**

3. Visibility: **Public or Unlisted** (never Private — judges cannot open it).
